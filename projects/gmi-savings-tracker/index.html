GMI Savings Tracker: case study

By Sierra Napier, MPA/MPH. Data as of Nov 13, 2026 (portal updated Nov 9, 2026).

Bottom line: payments to the vendors tied to GMI fell in 5 of the 12 areas checked. That is evidence, not proof. The reported savings are not verified, and the rest of the picture needs agency records.

Independent prototype. This is not an official State of Maryland product. It is built only from Maryland's open data portal (opendata.maryland.gov) through the SODA API. DBM budget books and State sites were used only to verify the numbers.

The problem

Maryland's Government Modernization Initiative (GMI) reports savings. The question here is simple: did those savings show up in what the State actually paid, and where could more be saved?

Two totals have been reported. They count different things, so this project keeps them apart and labels each one. Neither is verified here.

  • $29M. From the GMI final report to the Joint Chairmen, Jan 26, 2026. It describes four items as saved in FY2026.
  • $50M. From the Governor's GMI page, status as of June 2026, which says "implemented actions to save $50 million." The page does not say whether that is one-time or yearly.

The method

Build the data

Six State datasets, 1.29M rows in all, were downloaded and checked against the portal's own counts and totals. They were loaded into DuckDB in layers: raw, reviewed rules, core, and 62 SQL marts. The result is published as 77 tables and a static dashboard with 11 tabs.

Stop checks

143 of 143 stop checks pass. Nothing publishes unless every check passes.

Reconcile to official sources

  • Every actual year, FY2017 to FY2025, reconciles with DBM's printed budget books. Statewide totals are within 0.008%, and 99.3% to 100% of programs match to the dollar.
  • The enacted FY2027 budget equals DBM's Fiscal Digest in every fund.
  • 31 of 31 yearly totals equal the State's Open Finance sites to the cent.

Look for payment evidence

For each GMI area, the tracker looks at payments to the matching vendors before and after. It treats a drop as evidence to follow up on, not as proof of savings.

Model a path and find leads

A calculator carries GMI's reported rates forward and adds labeled assumptions. Opportunity detectors flag places where more could be saved. Forecast models are shown only if they beat a simple baseline on held-out data.

The result

Payment evidence

Payments to the matching vendors fell in 5 of the 12 areas checked. Two examples: office supplies fell 48% and vehicle purchases fell 26%. The other areas are newer and need agency records to confirm.

A conditional path

  • Carrying GMI's reported rates forward gives about $29.6M a year.
  • Add an assumed 3.5% saving (range 2% to 5%) on statewide contracts for 27 vendors, which had $569.3M in FY2026 payments, plus 1% starting savings in real estate and utilities.
  • The conditional path is then $52.6M, with a range of $42.6M to $64.2M.
  • About 14% of that path has payment evidence.

The assumptions are labeled and can be changed in the dashboard calculator.

Opportunity detectors

The detectors found 389 leads worth up to $330.5M a year ($219.7M likely). Leads overlap, so they cannot be added up, and they are not savings. As a check on usefulness, 76% of flagged units were flagged again the next year, compared with 5% for like-sized unflagged units.

Forecast ranges

Raw 80% ranges held 58.7% of 63 past outcomes. After split-conformal calibration, 53 of 63 held (84.1%, 95% interval 73% to 91%).

A source issue, reported and not a finding

The FY2022 payments data lists a second $874.5M block for Maryland Environmental Service and Maryland Transportation Authority.

The limits

  • Public payment data has no vendor number, only name and ZIP, so matching vendors is imperfect.
  • Payments are cash paid.
  • FY2027 is partial.
  • Payments are evidence, not proof of savings.
  • The $29M and $50M totals are neither verified here.
  • This is not an audit under government auditing standards.
